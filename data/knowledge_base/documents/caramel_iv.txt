Ingredient:
Caramel IV

Aliases:
Caramel 150d
Sulfite Ammonia Caramel
INS 150d
E150d

Category:
Color Additive

Function:
Provides brown color to foods.

Description:
The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India identifies Caramel IV as sulfite ammonia caramel and assigns it International Numbering System (INS) number 150d.

Regulatory Information:
India — FSSAI's Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations compendium, Version XXI dated 31 January 2022, lists Caramel IV (sulfite ammonia caramel, INS 150d) with category-specific maximum levels. Table 15.0 (ready-to-eat savouries) lists 10,000 mg/kg; the table's Table 15.1 subcategory for potato, cereal, flour or starch snacks does not list Caramel IV. The table entries are reported as published; this document does not infer whether the parent-category entry applies to a specific subcategory and does not confirm that no later amendment exists.

International scientific evaluation — JECFA's 1985 evaluation (29th meeting) records an ADI of 0–200 mg/kg body weight per day, with a comment that the ADI on a solids basis is 0–150 mg/kg body weight per day. This toxicological evaluation is not a regulatory maximum for a food category.

European Union scientific evaluation — EFSA's 2011 re-evaluation covered caramel colours E 150a, b, c and d. It established a group ADI of 300 mg/kg body weight per day for combined exposure to all four colours, with a lower specific ADI for E 150c within that group. EFSA noted that high consumers could exceed ADIs for E 150a, E 150c and E 150d at industry-reported maximum use levels. EFSA's 2012 refined exposure assessment used updated use and consumption data; neither evaluation measures the level in this product. EU legal conditions are maintained separately in the Commission's Union-list database.

Assessment Notes:
The evidence distinguishes category-specific Indian permissions from international/EU scientific evaluations. The amount of Caramel IV in the product is unknown, and Table 15.0 versus 15.1 applicability is not resolved here. Neither an ADI nor an entry in a broader category proves product compliance or actual consumer exposure.
